Price vs. Value: Why the Difference Matters

The market sets a stock's trading price. It does not determine a company's intrinsic value with certainty.

01. What drives a stock price?

A stock price reflects transactions between buyers and sellers. Expectations about earnings, interest rates, the economy, sentiment, and supply and demand can all influence it. Prices may move substantially even when business performance barely changes.

02. What is intrinsic value?

Intrinsic value is an estimate based on a company's expected future cash flows or earnings, assets, and competitive position. Different assumptions can produce different estimates, so intrinsic value cannot be observed as one indisputable number.

03. Mr. Market

Benjamin Graham's Mr. Market analogy describes a market participant offering changing prices, sometimes optimistic and sometimes pessimistic. Investors need not accept every quotation. They can judge whether the offered price is attractive.

04. A simple illustration

Suppose estimated intrinsic value is $100 per share and the stock can be purchased for $60. That represents a 40% discount to the estimate. But the $100 estimate could be wrong, and a discount does not guarantee a profit.

05. Cheap is not necessarily undervalued

A $5 stock is not automatically cheaper in economic terms than a $100 stock. Share count, earnings, assets, growth, and risk matter. Low valuation multiples may reflect genuine business problems.

Ask not only whether a stock price will rise, but what the business is worth relative to its current price.

A practical valuation cross-check

Suppose a company earns 100 units in a typical year. At 15 times earnings, the implied equity value is 1,500 units. But if sustainable earnings fall to 70, the same multiple implies only 1,050. The result changes even without any change in the multiple.

A robust valuation therefore compares normalized earnings, cash flow after necessary investment, debt and plausible future scenarios. Market price is observable; intrinsic value remains an estimate.
