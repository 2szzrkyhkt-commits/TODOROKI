Margin of Safety: Benjamin Graham's Core Principle

A margin of safety means leaving room for error between estimated intrinsic value and the price paid for an investment.

01. The basic concept

If a share is estimated to be worth $100, paying $60 rather than $100 creates a larger cushion against mistakes in the valuation. The cushion is based on an estimate, not on a guaranteed future sale price.

Dollar margin of safety = estimated intrinsic value − purchase price.
Percentage margin of safety = (estimated intrinsic value − purchase price) ÷ estimated intrinsic value × 100.

02. An example

At an estimated intrinsic value of $100, a purchase price of $90 implies a 10% margin, $75 implies 25%, $60 implies 40%, and $50 implies 50%.

03. Why uncertainty matters

Valuation depends on assumptions about future profits, growth, discount rates, and competitive conditions. An estimate of $100 might later prove closer to $75. Paying less may leave more room for such errors, but losses remain possible.

04. Financial strength

Benjamin Graham also emphasized the strength of a company's balance sheet and the stability of its earnings. Liquidity, debt obligations, assets, and sustained profitability can help investors assess downside risk.

05. Net-net investing

Net current asset value is commonly calculated as current assets minus total liabilities. A net-net approach looks for businesses trading at a substantial discount to that amount. Receivable collectability, inventory realizability, and future operating losses still require scrutiny.

06. Low multiples are not enough

A low P/E or P/B ratio does not establish a margin of safety by itself. Weak earnings, deteriorating assets, and poor cash generation can make a seemingly cheap stock expensive.

The goal is not perfect prediction. It is to estimate value carefully and avoid paying a price that leaves no room for being wrong.
