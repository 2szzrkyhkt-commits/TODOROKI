What Is Investing?

Investing is not simply betting on a rising stock price. It starts with understanding a business and deciding what its ownership is worth.

01. Investing versus speculation

Stock prices fluctuate every day, but the quoted price and a company's underlying value are not necessarily the same. Investing involves examining a company's finances, earning power, and prospects while seeking an appropriate return with attention to the safety of capital. Trading mainly to profit from price movements is more speculative.

02. Price and value

Even an excellent business can be a poor investment at an excessive price. Conversely, an undervalued business may offer an opportunity. Business quality and purchase price are separate questions.

03. Margin of safety

Intrinsic value is an estimate, not a certainty. Buying at a meaningful discount to that estimate provides room for analytical mistakes or disappointing performance. It does not eliminate the risk of loss.

04. Look beyond simple ratios

Price-to-earnings and price-to-book ratios alone cannot establish whether a stock is attractive. Earnings consistency, cash flow, financial strength, capital efficiency, competitive advantages, and changing business conditions all matter.

05. The central idea

Understand the business, estimate its value, and demand an adequate margin of safety before buying.
